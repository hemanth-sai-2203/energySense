CASE STUDY 01
Grid Transformation

The Vanishing Deficit: How India Conquered Structural Peak Shortages

A decade ago in 2013, India's power grid was plagued by frequent load shedding and severe transmission bottlenecks, with daily peak deficit averaging 5,685 MW and maximum recorded shortages reaching 12,251 MW (over 8.5% of total demand). By 2023, through the creation of the One Nation, One Grid synchronous interconnection, rapid transmission highway buildout, and over 150 GW of thermal and renewable additions, average peak deficits plummeted by 93.3% to just 383 MW (<0.2% of peak demand). Today, grid reliability stands at unprecedented historic highs.

2013 Avg Daily Deficit 5,686 MW
2023 Avg Daily Deficit 383 MW
Deficit Reduction -93.3%
Power Availability >99.7%
CASE STUDY 02
Economic Shocks

The COVID-19 Demand Shock & The Resilient V-Shaped Rebound

When India implemented the nationwide lockdown on March 24, 2020, industrial and commercial operations ceased overnight. Analysis of the daily energy data reveals an unprecedented 24.0% contraction in electricity consumption in April 2020 (falling from 3,733 MU/day in April 2019 to 2,838 MU/day). However, as manufacturing resumed and agricultural demand held firm, India staged a dramatic recovery: by April 2021, daily demand rebounded by 40.4% to 3,985 MU/day, surpassing pre-pandemic levels and highlighting the country's underlying economic momentum.

Pre-Lockdown (Apr 2019) 3,733 MU/day
Lockdown Dip (Apr 2020) 2,839 MU/day
Rebound (Apr 2021) 3,985 MU/day
Net Rebound +40.4% YoY
CASE STUDY 03
Clean Transition

The Solar Surge: Scaling from 46 TWh to 134 TWh in Five Years

India's renewable electricity journey represents one of the fastest solar deployments globally. Monthly generation data from Ember shows annual solar generation expanding from 46,267 GWh in 2019 to 133,795 GWh in 2024—a 2.89x increase. In July 2025, clean energy generation (solar, wind, hydro, and nuclear) set an all-time record, accounting for 35.74% of total monthly electricity generation. The states of Rajasthan and Gujarat have emerged as national clean power engines, each possessing over 20 GW of installed renewable generation.

2019 Solar Output 46.3 TWh
2024 Solar Output 133.8 TWh
Growth Multiple 2.89x
Peak Clean Share 35.7%
CASE STUDY 04
Climate & Grid Dynamics

Extreme Heat & Cooling Demand: The Temperature Elasticity of India's Grid

By merging the daily Universal Thermal Climate Index (UTCI) with grid demand, we observe a dramatic rise in peak electricity sensitivity to heatwaves. In peak summer months, each 1°C increase in thermal heat index adds approximately ~200 MW of immediate peak load nationally, driven by rapid urbanization and the proliferation of room air conditioning. As heatwaves intensify in frequency and duration across Northern and Central India, grid operators must plan for sharper evening cooling peaks as residential cooling coincides with solar sunset.
